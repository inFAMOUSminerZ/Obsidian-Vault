## Overview

Part One left the party with a handful of facts and the sense of being watched. Two people are dead without a mark on them. Ompech Paythas at his family mansion and the lizardfolk Jarock at Golden Glade Manor. Four graves belonging to the Saltback Six have been opened with care. A skull-headed figure has been glimpsed by some of the previous party and related NPC's. A dark clergy that dissolves into ash has tried to kill Sonder, calling her a life-thief, and a captured member whispered "Six debts."

Part Two will answer the question Part One left open. Who is the Pale Adjudicator, and why is he doing this? The answer is Levin, the healer of the Saltback Six and a cleric of Terra. The party will not be able to stop what he is building by force alone. They have to understand it first.

The quest is expected to run six to eight hours. This is the order the party should experience it in:

- **Act 1, the Letter and the Briefing:** The Helion Guard summons the party to its guildhall, reviews Part One’s evidence, and sends them to the Saltback Slums and House Paythas with a warning not to frighten anyone.
- **Act 2, the Sites of Interest:** The party investigates four sites in any order: the infirmary where Levin once worked, House Paythas, the Altus Templum of Terra in the city, and the Altar of Terra in the Cradle of Giants. The infirmary and House Paythas hold clues that lead to the basement beneath Ailments and Ale.
- **Act 3, the Confrontation:** The party goes to the basement beneath Ailments and Ale to confront Levin.
- **Not yet placed:** The meeting with the original Church of Proper Life comes mid-quest.

## Doompesh or Levin
By the end of Part One, the party will have narrowed the Adjudicator down to two names. The party can be sure of one thing. It is one of the Six who never lay in a disturbed grave. It is either Doompesh or Levin, and they could be mistaken about both.

### Third Option
If the party fixates on someone else, the natural candidate is Eamon Rusk, the half-blind groundskeeper. He is balding with white hair, knows every grave, and could not remember the cleric’s name. Eamon is not the Adjudicator, but he could secretly be one of Levin’s clergy. That is for the DM to decide, and the party has no reason to suspect it. If they lean on him, let him clear himself of being the Adjudicator in a single scene or perhaps a novelty encounter.

## Act 1
### OOC Description
The quest opens when a letter from the Helion Guard reaches each member of the party, requesting their presence at the Guard’s guildhall in the city for a briefing on where the investigation should continue. The Guard’s concern is for the comfort of the wealthy, not for the dead. The letter should make that clear: it relays a complaint from House Paythas about the party’s conduct and asks them not to frighten anyone at any site they visit.

**At the guildhall, Caelan Veyr briefs the party and sends them to two places.** 
1. **An infirmary in the Saltback Slums that the Six used to frequent, where Levin later worked.** 
2. **House Paythas, where the party is asked to be courteous.** 

The party may visit the two sites in either order. The Helion Guard are a private defence force of the Royal House of the Silversky. Play its members as highly respected and proud, but stretched. The Guard has expanded rapidly and is hiring people below its measure, and there is open frustration at having to. It treats a slum investigation as a distraction beneath it.

## Act 2
### OOC Description
The party has four sites of interest and may visit them in any order: the infirmary in the Saltback Slums, House Paythas, the Altus Templum of Terra in the city, and the Altar of Terra in the Cradle of Giants. Two of the sites hold clues that lead to the basement beneath Ailments and Ale: the infirmary and House Paythas. The party needs only one of them. Varesh’s parting words at House Paythas are what send the party to the Altar, so if the party never visits House Paythas, the infirmary’s caretaker can supply the same fact.

The infirmary is one the Six frequented when Levin was out of magic. Levin eventually joined it and made it more popular and better funded, and the war, which fills its beds with wounded, and the refugee camp on the slums’ edge, which sends its sick there too, have since grown it far beyond that. The Six’s history with it need not be mentioned directly. Soldiers of the Helion Guard are among the wounded, so a uniform is tolerated, but nobody has time for questions. Helping with the sick earns trust without a roll. Otherwise, a DC 14 Charisma (Persuasion) check earns a few minutes of the caretaker’s attention.

### Site of Interest One: The Slums Infirmary
#### IC Description
```
*The infirmary announces itself long before you reach it. A queue of the weary winds out of its door and down the lane: refugees from the conquered kingdoms with their lives bundled in blankets, soldiers with bandaged limbs leaning on one another, mothers carrying feverish children. Smoke and vinegar hang in the air, and beneath them the sweeter smell of crushed herbs.*

*Inside, what must once have been a single ward has swallowed the whole building. Cots line every room and spill into the corridors, so that you have to turn sideways to pass. Canvas lean-tos have been stretched across the courtyard, sagging under the damp, and beneath them lie more cots, more patients, more quiet groaning. Herbs hang to dry from every beam, and water boils in black kettles at three separate hearths. Volunteers in stained aprons hurry between the beds, most too busy to look up. Here and there a scrap of Helion colours shows among the bandages, soldiers brought back from the front, and nobody has the time to care.*

*In the oldest ward at the back, the noise thins. The walls here are older, the plaster stained with a century of lamp smoke, and the cots are fewer. On a windowsill, in a clay pot, a Bija seedling reaches for the light. Beside it, on the wall, is an empty hook worn smooth where a cloak once hung.*
```

#### IC Description: The Caretaker
```
*Among the chaos, one figure moves to a different rhythm. She is an elderly woman with grey hair twisted into a knot and sleeves rolled to the elbow, her forearms corded from years of lifting the sick. She never seems to hurry, but nothing she does is wasted: a hand to a forehead, a word to a volunteer, a glance that sends a kettle off the boil. Her eyes are sharp and tired, the eyes of someone who has seen everyone who comes through this door at their worst. #### Questions
**1. What was Levin like?**
```
*Her face softens before she speaks, as though a window has opened somewhere inside her. “Levin. Yes.” She smiles at the empty hook. “He charged what people could pay and never what they couldn’t. Half the slums owed him something, and he never once collected.” She shakes her head slowly. “The others used to argue about what was wrong with the world. Corruption, they’d say, or greed, or the powerful. Levin never argued. He’d listen, and then he’d say it was always somebody dying too soon.”*
```
**Outcome:** The party gets a picture of a gentle healer who could not accept death, which sets up his motive.

**2. Why did he stop coming?**
```
*The warmth drains from her face. “After the old dragonborn’s funeral,” she says. “He came in that evening, stood at that window for an hour, and in the morning his cloak was gone, and so was he.” Her gaze goes to the hook. “We thought it was grief. We told ourselves it would pass. That was years ago.”*
```
**Outcome:** Levin’s withdrawal began with Doompesh’s death, and the empty hook marks where his Terra cloak hung.

**3. Tell us about Doompesh.**
```
*She almost laughs, a short, wet sound. “Doompesh stood at the door of this place for decades. You’d never meet a steadier soul.” She folds her arms. “He made Levin promise never to bring him back, and Levin kept it. When the end came, Levin sat with him and held his hand until morning.” Her eyes sharpen. “If you think Doompesh would walk out of the grave to hurt anyone, you didn’t know him. He’d have called it an insult.”*
```
**Outcome:** This confirms that Doompesh refused resurrection and cannot be the Adjudicator. A party still suspecting him should see that he would never return.

**4. Did Levin keep records of his work?**
```
*She considers it, glancing over her shoulder at the crowded ward. “He wrote everything down. Every patient, every night, always at that window.” She nods toward the sill. “I never read it. It wasn’t mine to read. But I’d have looked under the boards before I let anyone throw it out.”*
```
**Outcome:** The caretaker’s hint points the party to the loose floorboard beneath the window. With the hint, the casebook is found automatically.

**5. What can you tell us about Bija trees?**
```
*She glances at the seedling on the sill, and her expression turns fond and a little rueful. “Levin brought that one in. He said a healer should keep one close.” She taps the clay pot. “The old trees are a wonder. Leaf poultices that knit a wound faster than they should, bark tea for fevers, and the sap, if you know how to use it, does more than that. We use every scrap.” Her voice lowers, as if she is passing on something she was told in confidence. “Levin was a cleric of Terra, you know. He told me once that the Bija trees were Terra’s first intervention, the very first thing the god ever did with his own hands.” She smiles and shakes her head. “Then he laughed and said it was only a legend, even to the priests. He said Terra doesn’t explain himself.”*
```
**Outcome:** The party learns what Bija trees are used for, that Levin brought the seedling in himself, and the legend that Terra created the Bija trees as his first intervention, which even Levin treated as a legend. This links the seedling to Levin’s faith and prepares the blood-offering checks below.

#### IC Descriptions
**DC 12 Intelligence (Investigation):** Tucked behind a shelf of poultices is a slate covered in chalk, a child’s drawing of six figures standing in a row. You pick out a dragonborn at one end, unmistakable and drawn in blue chalk, and at the other a tall, balding man in a green-brown cloak who is plainly human.

**DC 15 Intelligence (Investigation), or automatic if the party follows the caretaker’s hint:** A loose board beneath the window gives way, and under it lies a thick casebook, its pages dense with names, dates, and results. You count four entries belonging to the Six. The last entries are in a later, harsher ink, and beside each is a single word: “owed.” Tucked inside the back cover is a hand-drawn plan of a cellar: a narrow stair, and six marks arranged in a ring around a seventh, with a tankard-and-cross tavern sign inked beside it and a single word, “below.” The party can place it as the cellar of Ailments and Ale.

**DC 13 Wisdom (Medicine or Survival), on the seedling:** The soil around the roots is stained a dull rust. Looking closer, you find many small offerings, the oldest long dry and brown, the newest only days old.

**DC 14 Intelligence (Religion):** Blood given to a Bija tree is an old devotion to Terra, the practice of proving your nature. Someone has been offering themselves at this windowsill for years.

**Kin, DC 14 Wisdom (Perception):** As your eyes drift toward the windowsill, a pale shape stands beside it for a moment, motionless and almost forgotten by the room. The instant you look at it directly, it is gone.

### Site of Interest Two: House Paythas
#### OOC Description
The party can return to House Paythas to speak with Varesh, the child who first described his grandfather’s friend. All of House Paythas is dragonborn with blue scales, which marks them as Doompesh’s family. The family is grieving and wary of strangers, which the Guard’s letter has already made worse. Reaching Varesh means getting past the household guards at the gate and then his parents, who receive the party on the front steps and do not invite them inside. The decision is not theirs to make. Doompesh’s widow, Surina, is the head of the house, and she is the one who allows the visit.

Courtesy (condolences, a mention of the Guard’s instructions, a quiet voice) makes Surina’s judgement a DC 13 Charisma (Persuasion) check. Rudeness or threats raise it to DC 16, and Caelan hears about it. The check changes how warmly Surina treats the party and how closely she watches, not the outcome. Varesh asks to speak with the party either way, and Surina allows three questions before she ends the visit. If the party presses past three, she ends it herself.

Varesh is Ompech’s first cousin and, like Ompech, a grandson of Doompesh and Surina. He is fifteen, which is young for a dragonborn, and he looks closer to ten. He is calmer than when the party first met him, because the events have had time to settle in his mind. Below are four expected questions. If the party asks something else, answer it in the same spirit, with a small clue that points the same way.

#### Surina Paythas
Surina is Doompesh’s widow and the head of House Paythas. Ompech and Varesh are both her grandsons, and she is grieving Ompech, though she shows it only in how still she holds herself. She is old for a dragonborn, with blue scales faded to the grey-blue of a winter sky, a worn crest, and pale gold eyes. She walks with a blackwood cane and wears a plain steel ring on a chain at her throat, which was her husband’s. She is formal, exact and unhurried, and she has never needed to raise her voice. She has no patience for the Guard’s condescension, and she does not take instruction from people who stand behind desks.

She knew Levin. He was her husband’s healer and the great love of his later life, and she accepted that long ago. She is not afraid of what has been visiting the house, and she is not ashamed of it, which sets her apart from her son, who would rather the Saltback years were never mentioned. She allows the visit because she wants Varesh to be heard, and because she wants to know what her husband’s friend is doing.

In play, she watches from a stone bench and says little. She will not name Levin unless the party names him first. If they do, she does not confirm or deny it, but she does not look surprised.

### IC Description: The Burning
```
*At some point he stops counting. He lays one hand flat in the chalk of the seventh mark, and the ring begins to burn. There is no heat.
